1What changed this week
Two genuinely new things this week. First, our AI-answer visibility is finally baselined: Slingshot appears in 3.1% of tracked LLM answers (Amplitude AI Visibility, report completed Jul 28) versus Asana 46.9%, Monday 40.6% and ClickUp 25% — hard confirmation that the awareness gap extends into how AI assistants recommend tools. Second, the competitive story has shifted from "everyone shipped free AI agents" to "everyone is re-pricing AI from free to metered and shrinking free tiers" — Notion moved agents to credits ($10/1,000), Wrike capped AI Elite at 300 pooled actions/mo, and Airtable quietly cut its free base from 1,200 to 1,000 records. Meanwhile ClickUp bet the company on AI: a 22% layoff (~290 people) paired with 3,000 internal agents and million-dollar "100x" salary bands. SEO and paid footprints are essentially flat WoW — the ~1,000× reach gap is structural, not moving.

2Cadence read
The 2026 AI-agent land-grab is entering its monetization phase. Six months ago every rival shipped autonomous agents and several gave them away; now Notion, Wrike and Airtable are metering AI and trimming free tiers, and ClickUp has restructured its entire org around agents. Two implications for Slingshot: (1) "we have agents too" was never the winning line, and now "our AI won't nickel-and-dime you" is available as a genuine contrast; (2) the durable differentiator is still "our AI acts on your connected data." Slingshot's native analytics + data connections let its AI answer business questions ("which campaign is underperforming and who owns it?") and turn the answer into a task, in one place — the one AI story pure work-management tools structurally can't tell without bolting on a BI layer first. Lean into it, and add the pricing contrast on top.

The AI-first restructuring wave arrives: ClickUp cut 22% of staff (~290) on May 21, deployed ~3,000 internal agents at a 3:1 agent-to-employee ratio, and introduced $1M "100x" salary bands — one of the first public SaaS layoffs explicitly framed as agent-substitution, not cost-cutting. Standing context: ClickUp ~$4B / ~$300M ARR (IPO rumored); Asana (NYSE:ASAN) acquired StackAI ($75M, May 2026); Monday (NASDAQ:MNDY) FY25 revenue $1.23B (+27%); Smartsheet taken private by Blackstone/Vista ($8.4B, closed Jan 2025); Notion ~$11B secondary / ~$600M ARR; Airtable marked down $11.7B → ~$4B (Jan 2026). Net signal: the category is maturing, AI is shifting from free to metered, and pricing/valuation/stability pressure is opening a value-and-focus lane for a differentiated challenger.
