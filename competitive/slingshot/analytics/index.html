1The state of play — first edition
First edition — baseline. No prior report to diff against; from next week we lead with what changed. On the analytics side, Slingshot competes with two groups: marketing-dashboard specialists (Databox, DashThis, AgencyAnalytics, Geckoboard, Klipfolio) and enterprise BI (Looker, Power BI, Looker Studio). The specialists win on connector breadth (85–200+ integrations) and demand capture — AgencyAnalytics alone drives ~12K paid visits/mo; the BI players win on depth. Slingshot's edge is neither breadth nor depth — it's that the insight and the work live in the same tool: a Slingshot dashboard's number becomes a task or a discussion without an export. Paired with a genuine $0 free tier while the field drifts toward metered/consumption pricing (Databox per-source fees, DashThis source-based, Looker/Power BI token & Fabric metering), that's a real, ownable wedge for mid-market marketing teams.

2Cadence read
Two forces are shaping the category: AI-native data access and metered pricing. Databox's MCP server is the notable move — it lets an LLM query dashboard data directly, which is exactly the "ask your data a question" behavior buyers now expect. Where this pressures us: we need a visible MCP/AI-query story (Slingshot already has an MCP surface — say so). Where the category hands us an opening: everyone is drifting to consumption/metered pricing (Databox per-source, DashThis source-based, Looker/Power BI token & Fabric metering) while Slingshot stays flat with a real free tier — and, crucially, none of them let you act on the insight without switching tools. That "dashboard → task → discussion" loop is the one thing not on any competitor's roadmap.

2Honest read
We win on the act-on-insight loop, pricing/free tier, and marketing-execution fit — a marketing team that wants dashboards and a place to do the work gets both in one tool, free to start. We have three real gaps: (1) connector breadth vs Databox's 200+ / AgencyAnalytics' 85+, the most common deal-blocker; (2) white-label client reporting, where DashThis and AgencyAnalytics are purpose-built for agencies; and (3) enterprise BI depth vs Looker/Power BI (we're not, and shouldn't pretend to be, a semantic-modeling BI engine). The honest positioning: we're not the deepest dashboard tool or the biggest connector list — we're the only one where the insight turns into action without leaving the app, at a price that starts free.

Verdict — the one response that matters this week

Own the one thing no reporting tool has: dashboards you can act on. Slingshot won't win connector breadth against Databox or agency client-reporting against AgencyAnalytics, and it shouldn't try to be enterprise BI. Its defensible position is the loop the entire category ignores — a dashboard number becomes a task and a discussion in the same tool, starting free while rivals meter by source, widget and token. Make that loop the headline of every analytics touchpoint, wrap it in a flat-vs-metered pricing story, and back it with the MCP/"ask your data" proof point. That's the wedge that turns a tiny challenger into a category of one.
