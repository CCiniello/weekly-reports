Executive summary — post to Slingshot

Reveal SEO: we've built the visibility — now we convert it

Two-line version: Reveal is winning in AI search and ranking in Google, but our commercial pages are stuck on page 2 where nobody clicks. The work now is execution, not strategy.

What's working

AI search is a real strength. After rebuilding how we track it, Reveal appears in about 1 in 4 AI answers on embedded analytics (27% visibility), with 80% positive sentiment and solid presence on both Google AI Overview (38%) and ChatGPT (18%). As buyers move to AI-first research, that's an asset.

What's not

Our money pages rank but sit on page 2 (embedded analytics ~#17, white label ~#15) — close, but page 2 earns almost no clicks. And technical site health is still 36/100 with thousands of erroring URLs, which caps how high anything can climb.

The priority this week
  • Ship two product pages — /embedded-bi (its terms only rank via the homepage today) and /embedded-dashboards (already ~#9, quickest win).
  • Optimize the page-2 pages — titles, internal links and content to cross onto page 1.
  • Clear the technical errors — dev shipped 2 fixes; the P1 subdomain/canonical/403 set is queued.
For leadership

Demand is steady but modest — organic leads are running ~24/mo (deduped by email), about a quarter of the 100/mo goal. This is a conversion-and-execution story: the demand and AI visibility are there; the constraint is getting pages onto page 1. As that happens, leads should follow on the usual lag.

Two things live here: (1) our source of truth — which tool is the authority for each number and why, so nobody compares two tools that measure different things; and (2) a plain-English SEO glossary so anyone reading the report knows what the terms mean. The rule underneath it all: one metric, one tool — a trend is only trustworthy if it comes from the same place every week.

SEO glossary — plain English
TermWhat it meansWhere / why we track it
KeywordA word or phrase people type into search (e.g. “embedded analytics”). We group related keywords into clusters around one page.Ahrefs for volume; GSC for how we rank.
Head term vs. long-tailA head term is short and high-volume (“embedded analytics”). A long-tail keyword is longer, more specific and lower-volume (“best embedded analytics for SaaS startups”) — less traffic each, but easier to rank for and higher intent.Ahrefs (find them) + GSC (track them).
ImpressionsHow many times our page showed up in search results for a query — whether or not anyone clicked.GSC.
Clicks & CTRClicks = people who actually clicked through. CTR (click-through rate) = clicks ÷ impressions. Low CTR at a good position usually means a weak title or an AI Overview taking the click.GSC.
Position / rankingWhere our page sits in Google results for a query. Page 1 = positions 1–10 (almost all clicks); page 2 = 11–20 (almost none).GSC (ours); Ahrefs (competitors).
BacklinkA link from another website to ours. Backlinks are votes of confidence — more good ones generally means Google trusts the site more.Ahrefs.
Domain Rating (DR)Ahrefs' 0–100 score of a site's overall backlink strength / authority. Higher DR makes it easier to rank. Ours is 55; goal is 60.Ahrefs.
CanonicalA tag that tells Google “this is the official version of this page” when duplicates exist. Broken canonicals confuse Google about which page to rank.Ahrefs Site Audit.
Technical healthA crawl-based score (0–100) of how clean the site is — broken links, errors, redirects, sitemaps. Low health caps how high anything can rank.Ahrefs Site Audit.
AI visibility (AEO)How often AI answers (Google AI Overview, ChatGPT) mention our brand for relevant questions — the AI-era version of ranking. AEO = Answer Engine Optimization.Amplitude AI Visibility.
Share of voiceOf all the AI answers to a set of questions, the % that mention a given brand — how loud we are vs. competitors in AI answers.Amplitude.
Cluster / PAGE GAPA cluster is a group of related keywords one page should own. PAGE GAP means we have the demand but no dedicated page yet (e.g. embedded BI).GSC + Ahrefs.
The one rule: one metric, one tool — never blend sources for the same number. When two tools disagree, they're usually measuring two different things (search vs. on-site, our real data vs. a model's estimate), not contradicting each other.
