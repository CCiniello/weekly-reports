SECTION 2

What changed since last crawl

A lot of cleanup landed — but one root cause backfilled every gain, so the totals look flat.

  • Broken redirects: 568 → 16 — redirects that used to dead-end in an error are almost all fixed.
  • Canonical points to redirect: 180 → 9 — pages naming a redirect as their "official version" are nearly cleared.
  • 3XX redirects 1,610 → 25 and 4XX pages 1,143 → 66 — the trailing-slash duplicate redirects have been collapsed.
  • No hard 404s and no 500s this crawl — the old help-centre .html 500 errors flagged last month are gone.
  • "Not all pages in an hreflang group were crawled": 0 → 1,064 — brand new and the single biggest mover. A direct symptom of the locale 403 block: Google can't reach the language alternates, so it sees every language group as half-broken.
  • ~1,100 URLs now sit in the 4xx/5xx bucket, overwhelmingly /ja /es /ko /pt-BR paths returning 403. As redirect/4xx errors retired, these blocked locale pages moved in — which is why total errors edged up +9 instead of dropping.

Where the errors actually come from — one dominant root cause and two small ones:

1. Blocked language pages (the dominant driver)

The Japanese, Spanish, Korean and Portuguese sections (/ja/ /es/ /ko/ /pt-BR/) return "403 Forbidden" — a locked door — on ~1,100 URLs the site itself links to. This one fault also produces this week's hreflang spike, the broken-redirect stragglers (locale URLs that 301 to a 403), and most of the sitemap junk.

2. A malformed "In the News" pagination bug

A template is building pagination links with the query part mangled — an encoded %3F, a second ?, and a stray &fr=1 — inventing URLs that don't exist and 403 dead-end. Small in count, but self-inflicted.

3. Orphan pages ticking up

Pages with no internal links pointing to them rose from 43 to 47 (4 new). Minor, but worth a quick internal-linking pass.

Is this our fault? Yes — both the wins and the remaining pain are ours. The wins are our redirect/slash work paying off. The 403 block is a server/CDN configuration on our own site refusing access to the locale sections — not a Google penalty. It's fully in our control, and it's the one thing holding the score down.

SECTION 6

Cheat sheet — plain-English glossary

What the terms in this report actually mean.

Crawl
When a search engine's robot visits your pages to read them. Our audit is a crawl by Ahrefs' robot.
Health score
Ahrefs' 0–100 rating of how clean the site looks technically. Higher is better; 46 means lots of fixable debt.
404 (Not Found)
The page doesn't exist. A dead link.
403 (Forbidden)
The page exists but the server refuses to show it — a locked door. Worse than a 404 because we're blocking our own content.
500 (Server Error)
The page broke while loading — the server crashed on it.
Redirect (301 / 302)
Sends a visitor from an old URL to a new one. 301 = permanent (passes ranking value); 302 = temporary.
Broken redirect
A redirect that ends at a dead or forbidden page instead of a working one.
Redirect chain
Several redirects in a row before reaching the real page. Slows crawling and leaks value.
Canonical
A tag naming the "official" version of a page when duplicates exist, so Google credits the right one.
hreflang
A tag telling Google which language/country version of a page to show. Broken ones confuse it.
Indexing
A page being stored in Google's library so it can appear in results. No index = no traffic.
Crawl budget
The limited number of pages Google will crawl per visit. Broken URLs waste it.
Orphan page
A page with no internal links pointing to it, so it's hard to find and rarely crawled.
Trailing slash
The "/" at the end of a URL. With and without it count as two different pages unless you standardise.
