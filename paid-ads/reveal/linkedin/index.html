1 The read for leadership

Are we getting ROI, and is the channel healthy? The honest answer: LinkedIn is buying us high-quality attention from exactly the right people, very efficiently — but we can't yet prove it turns into pipeline, and that gap is this week's priority.

What we're achieving For about $1,150 a week we put Reveal in front of ~3,900 senior decision-makers at mid-market tech companies — founders, CEOs, CTOs, and VPs of Engineering and Product. Engagement is strong and improving: more of them clicked this week than last (+17%), at a lower cost per click ($1.09, down 10%). The audience is on-target and getting tighter week over week. In plain terms, the top of the funnel is healthy and the money is working hard.

Where we can't claim ROI yet — and I want to be straight We cannot currently connect a single dollar of this spend to a lead or an opportunity. Two reasons: we aren't running a lead-capture form, so LinkedIn hands us no leads directly; and the tracking on our ad links is broken — of 1,051 clicks this week, our analytics saw 8. Any pipeline LinkedIn is influencing is being quietly credited to other channels. The performance is almost certainly real — we just can't measure it, which for a spending decision is the same as not having it.

The call Fix it, don't cut it. The audience quality and efficiency have earned the right to be measured properly before we judge the channel. Turn on link tracking and a lead form, trim ~$450/week of clearly wasted spend, and within two weeks I can put a real cost-per-lead and ROI figure in front of you.

3 Messaging & ad copy — what to keep, what to refresh
Keep — build-vs-buy cost/risk5.5–8.0% CTR
Copy: "Building embedded analytics in-house now runs into a multi-year, six-figure commitment — and the costs that actually kill you (tenancy, AI, compliance, maintenance) never show up in the prototype."
Why it works: it names the exact hidden costs a CTO fears — multi-tenancy, AI grounding, compliance — so it reads as insider truth, not a pitch. This is our sharpest angle. Refresh: remove "See the real numbers, July 21"; point to an evergreen cost breakdown or ROI calculator.
Keep — engineer-opportunity-cost4.5% CTR
Copy: "Your users keep asking for dashboards. Your engineers keep getting pulled off core product to build them."
Why it works: problem-first, ICP-specific, and speaks to the VP Eng / product-leader tension of protecting roadmap time. Strong opener worth reusing across formats.
Refresh — event & demo copy0.6–2.9% CTR
Copy: "Your seat is still open — July 21" and "You've seen what Reveal can do. Now see it running…"
Why it fails: the first is an expired deadline; the second assumes prior awareness we don't have with a cold audience. Refresh: retire the event CTAs, and for cold traffic swap the demo ask for a lower-friction offer (the cost breakdown asset or a 15-min product tour).
Add — CTO proof pointopportunity
Gap: CTOs and VP Eng are our highest-CTR titles (6.0%), yet no single ad leads on the hardest technical objections — security, multi-tenant data isolation, compliance. Why it matters: a proof-led unit (e.g., "SOC 2, row-level multi-tenant security, out of the box") could convert our most valuable, most engaged segment. Worth a test.

Verdict

FIX — do not scale yet, do not cut. The fundamentals are good and improving: efficient CPC/CTR and a genuinely on-ICP audience (founders, CTOs, VP Eng/Product at mid-market tech), tightening further week over week. But the channel is operationally blind — broken UTM tracking hides ~99% of clicks from GA4 and Salesforce, no lead-gen form exists, and ~$450/wk leaks into expired creative, dead retargeting and off-ICP audiences. Fix tracking (UTMs + a lead form) and trim the waste over the next 1–2 weeks, then re-evaluate for scale with real cost-per-lead in hand. Cutting now would kill a channel that's working before we've ever measured it.
