Read for leadership

ROI this week: unproven — and that’s the headline. We spent $1,092 on LinkedIn and drove 1,054 clicks at a strong 4.7% CTR, but the program captured zero leads and zero attributable pipeline. That isn’t a demand problem; it’s a plumbing problem. Every campaign is running a website-visit / conversion objective — there is no lead-gen form to capture anyone — and the paid clicks aren’t UTM-tagged, so GA4 and Salesforce literally cannot see the traffic.

Is the channel healthy? On media quality, yes. Unique reach grew 38% to 5,718 people while average frequency dropped from 5.5 to 3.9, and CPM fell 11% to $48.70. We reached more of the right people, more efficiently, with less ad fatigue. 64% of impressions hit our 201–1,000-employee mid-market sweet spot and 57% landed inside Tech / IT — the ICP is intact.

What we’re achieving: top-of-funnel awareness against the right accounts — Rocketlane, DoiT, DriveCentric, Open Dental and other SaaS/ISV names all saw us this week. The two “Ship embedded analytics” ads (image at 4.9% CTR, video at 7.5%) are doing real work. What we can’t yet prove: that any of this reach becomes a lead, a demo, or a dollar — because we’re not capturing or tracking it.

The call: FIX, don’t cut. Keep funding the working TOF ICP units, kill the dead retargeting ad ($210/wk at 0.26% CTR), and this week stand up a LinkedIn lead-gen form plus UTM tagging so we can finally attribute the channel. Until measurement exists, LinkedIn is a well-run awareness play we’re flying blind on.

What’s working / what’s not

Working

  • Reach up 38% (5,718) with frequency down to 3.9 — broader audience, less fatigue.
  • CPM down 11% to $48.70; CPC down to $1.04 — media buying more efficient WoW.
  • 4.7% CTR — roughly 10× LinkedIn’s benchmark; the ICP message resonates.
  • 64% of impressions in the 201–1,000 sweet spot; 57% in Tech / IT — ICP intact.
  • Video ad pulling 7.5% CTR — the strongest single creative in the account.

Not working

  • Zero leads: no lead-gen form exists — every campaign is a website-visit objective.
  • Untracked: paid clicks aren’t UTM-tagged; GA4/Salesforce see ~0 paid sessions.
  • Retargeting ad wasted $210 for 7 clicks (0.26% CTR) — effectively dead.
  • Senior-exec share (CXO/VP/Dir) slipped 2.7pp WoW while Entry-level doubled.
  • Engagement rate eased to 4.75% — minor, but worth watching against creative age.
ICP verdict
Are we buying the right people?

Mostly yes — the core is solid, but the edges are drifting. 63.5% of impressions hit the 201–1,000-employee mid-market sweet spot, 57% of industry impressions are Tech / IT, and technical functions (Engineering, Product, IT) make up ~39% of reach. That is a well-aimed embedded-analytics audience.

The watch item: senior-decision-maker share is easing. CXO + VP + Director fell from 42.8% to 40.1% of impressions week over week, while Entry-level doubled from 2.8% to 5.7% — the least valuable segment for a technical B2B buy. Senior (individual-contributor technical leads) rose +3.0pp, which is fine, but we want the economic buyers holding steady, not slipping. Tighten seniority targeting to defend the CXO/VP/Director tier.

Winning angle
What’s pulling?

The “build-vs-buy / free your engineers” angle is the account’s engine. Both top ads open on the customer’s pain (“your users keep asking for dashboards; your engineers keep getting pulled off core product”) and resolve to Reveal as the ship-fast alternative. The video cut is the standout at 7.52% CTR — 55% higher than the image version — and deserves more budget. The image ad is the volume workhorse (16.5K impr, 4.85% CTR).

The retargeting unit is the opposite story: a “book a demo” message to warm audiences pulling 0.26% CTR, effectively dead. Copy is on-message and ICP-aligned; no expired-date or seasonal language is running. One small inconsistency to fix: the two lead ads promise different timelines — “in Weeks” vs “in Days.” Standardize the claim.

Messaging & ad copy
Winner — Video · 7.52% CTR

“Your users keep asking for dashboards. Your engineers keep getting pulled off core product to build them… drop white-label dashboards, reporting, and AI-powered insights directly into your app, without the quarters-long build… See it running in 15 minutes.”

Why it works: names the exact ICP tension (eng opportunity cost), quantifies the payoff (“15 minutes”), and the “AI-powered insights” line rides current demand. Motion earns the extra CTR. Do: scale spend and build a lead-gen form variant behind it.

Workhorse — Image · 4.85% CTR

“‘Can we get analytics in the app?’ — your customers. Building it yourself means quarters of eng time and a maintenance burden that never ends. Reveal is embedded analytics built for product teams who’d rather ship than maintain…”

Why it works: the customer-quote hook is instantly relatable to product leaders; “ship than maintain” is crisp positioning. Fix: align the headline timeline with the video (“Days” vs “Weeks”) so the promise is consistent across the set.

Dead — Retargeting · 0.26% CTR

“You’ve seen what Reveal can do. Now see it running inside an app like yours… No commitment. Just answers.”

Why it fails: assumes prior awareness the retargeting pool may not have, offers a demo (high-commitment) with no proof or incentive, and the creative is stale for the audience. Do: pause it; if retargeting resumes, lead with a low-friction asset (interactive demo, ROI/build-vs-buy guide) gated behind a lead-gen form, not a raw “book a demo.”

The tracking gap (read this first)
Why the numbers on this tab are tiny

LinkedIn drove 1,054 paid clicks this week. GA4 recorded essentially zero of them as paid sessions. A source/medium filter for “linkedin” returns just 9 sessions — 5 organic-social and 4 referral — and 0 tagged “linkedin / cpc” sessions (prior week had exactly 1). The paid landing URLs have no UTM parameters, so GA4 attributes the traffic to “(direct)” or drops it, and Salesforce inherits the same blindness. The volume below is real organic/referral spillover, not the paid program.

Fix: append ?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign=… to every ad destination URL. This one change makes the entire channel visible in GA4 and Salesforce and is the prerequisite for any ROI claim.

Can we tie spend to pipeline?
Honest answer: not yet.

Right now there is no path from a LinkedIn dollar to a Salesforce opportunity. The channel has no lead-gen form (so no lead is captured on-platform) and no UTM tags (so GA4 and Salesforce can’t see the click). Any Reveal opp that touched LinkedIn this week would be credited to “(direct)” or last-touch brand search. We are spending ~$1.1K/week into a measurement black hole.

The good news: everything needed to fix it is a configuration change, not a strategy change. Add a lead-gen form to the two winning ads and UTM-tag all destination URLs, and within a week we can report leads, cost-per-lead, and first-touch influence on pipeline.
