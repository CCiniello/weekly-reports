Diagnosis (keyword-level data): across every Non-Brand-US ad group, landing-page experience is "Below average" on 100% of keywords and predicted CTR is "Below average" almost everywhere, while ad relevance is mostly "Average." 2 · Search visibility & the auction

Impression Share IS
The share of available ad impressions we actually won for a keyword. IS = our impressions ÷ total impressions we were eligible for. 80% IS means we appeared 8 of every 10 times someone could have seen us.
Top IS
Same idea, but only counting the premium slots above the organic results. This is the one we target (≥ 80% on head terms) — being on the page but below the fold barely gets clicks.
Search Lost IS (budget)
The share of impressions we lost because we ran out of money. If this is high on a high-intent term, the fix is literally "add budget" — a lever, not a problem.
Search Lost IS (rank)
The share we lost because our ad rank wasn't high enough (not money). The fix here is Quality Score, bid, or ad relevance — not budget. Splitting these two tells us exactly which knob to turn.
Quality Score QS
Google's 1–10 rating of how relevant our ad + landing page are to the keyword. Higher QS = we pay less and rank higher. When we lose IS to "rank," QS is usually the real lever — often fixed by better landing pages and ad copy, which is why the missing pages matter so much.
Ad Rank
The score Google uses to order ads in the auction. Roughly bid × Quality Score (plus extras). You can climb it by bidding more or by raising Quality Score.
CPC / CTR / CPM
CPC = cost per click. CTR = click-through rate (clicks ÷ impressions), a relevance signal. CPM = cost per 1,000 impressions, used for awareness channels where we pay for views, not clicks.

3 · Campaign types

Search campaign
Text ads on Google/Bing results pages, triggered by keywords. Our core engine — highest intent, because the person is actively searching for what we sell.
Demand Gen "DemandGen" campaign
Google's audience-based campaign type that runs image/video ads across YouTube, Discover, and Gmail to create awareness (vs. capturing existing search demand). Powerful when tiered by intent and pointed at the right page — wasteful when it dumps traffic on the homepage, which is our current issue.
Performance Max PMax
A single automated campaign where Google spreads spend across every surface (Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, Maps) using its own AI. Convenient but a black box — hard to see where money goes, so we watch it closely.
Responsive Search Ad RSA
The modern search ad format: you give Google multiple headlines and descriptions and it mixes the best-performing combination per search. "New ad copy" almost always means writing RSA assets.
Remarketing
Ads shown to people who already visited revealbi.io. Cheap, high-converting reminders — especially valuable for us given the long research window.
Smart Bidding / Maximize Conversions
Automated bidding where Google sets each bid to win the most conversions for the budget. Important for builds: on these campaigns you cannot hand-set bids — the lever is Quality Score and budget, not manual bid raises. Never wire a "raise the bid to hit 100% IS" action against a Smart Bidding campaign.
Negative keywords
A block-list of search terms we refuse to pay for (irrelevant or spammy queries). The main tool for keeping cheap channels like Bing clean.

4 · Keywords & targeting

Brand term
Searches for us by name — "reveal bi", "revealbi". Cheap and high-converting; we defend these at ~100% IS so competitors can't poach our own traffic ("brand defense").
Non-brand term
Category searches that don't mention us — "embedded analytics", "white label dashboards". This is where new demand lives and where winning is hard; our head-term goals live here.
Head term / head cluster
The highest-volume, most valuable non-brand keywords (and the group of close variants around them). Our "money terms" — e.g. embedded analytics, embedded BI, white-label analytics, embedded dashboards.
Conquest / competitor-intent
Bidding on searches about a competitor — "power bi embedded alternative", "<competitor> pricing" — to catch buyers comparing tools. Paired with a "Reveal vs. <competitor>" landing page.
ICP ideal customer profile
Who we actually want: SaaS companies & ISVs that need to embed analytics, and the people who buy it (CTOs / technical execs / developers). A lead outside ICP isn't a quality lead no matter how cheap.
Landing page / destination
The page an ad sends the click to. Match matters: a "white label" ad should land on the white-label page, not the homepage. Wrong destinations tank Quality Score and conversions — our current homepage problem.

5 · Funnel & measurement

Engagement rate GA4
In Google Analytics 4, the share of visits that were "engaged" (lasted 10s+, hit a conversion, or viewed 2+ pages). GA4 replaced the old "bounce rate" with this — higher is better.
Frequency LinkedIn / social
Average number of times the same person saw our ad. Too low = no memory; too high = fatigue and wasted spend. We watch it to know when creative needs a refresh.
First-touch vs. last-touch
Two ways to credit a conversion. First-touch credits the ad that discovered the buyer; last-touch credits the final click before converting. We report both so high-intent search isn't starved just because brand takes last-click credit.
30-day research lag Ours
Reveal is a high-consideration purchase — buyers often discover us on a non-brand search and convert 30+ days later on a brand search. So we never cut a capture campaign just because it shows zero same-week conversions.
WoW / MTD
WoW = week over week (this week vs. last). MTD = month to date (start of month through today). The report's default comparison is WoW, with 4-week trends for thin channels.
