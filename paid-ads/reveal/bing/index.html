1Executive summary
Bing held spend flat at ~$703 (+2% WoW) but bought 31% more clicks — 416 vs 318 — as avg CPC fell 22% to $1.69. The efficiency came from reining in the Compete-English-International campaign (CPC halved, $8.83→$4.38) and scaling Compete-US impression share from 70% to 83%. Quality traffic followed: GA4 engagement rate on bing/cpc rose to 71% (from 62%) and session duration jumped 48%. The catch is conversion visibility: the Bing pixel recorded just 1 conversion (a brand click) while GA4 saw 4 key events from bing/cpc — and Salesforce has no clean Bing LeadSource to confirm any of it. The channel is directionally working, but we are flying partially blind on ROI. Verdict: Fix & tighten before scaling (see Journey & ROI).

3Salesforce attribution gap
Read this before trusting any CPL number. Salesforce has no clean Bing LeadSource, so we cannot confirm a single Bing-sourced lead or dollar of pipeline in the CRM. We are triangulating from three disagreeing sources: Bing pixel = 1 conversion, GA4 bing/cpc = 4 key events (last-click), Salesforce = unverifiable. GA4 key events are form-fills/goals, not confirmed MQLs or opps. So the honest ROI picture is: Bing is buying engaged, on-ICP traffic to comparison pages, and some conversion signal is appearing — but we cannot yet tie it to revenue. One data-hygiene note: GA4 also shows a stray bing / CPC (capitalized) source with 4 sessions — a UTM casing inconsistency that fragments reporting and should be standardized to lowercase.
Verdict — Fix

Keep the channel on, but fix before you scale. The fundamentals moved the right way this week — CPC down 22%, clicks up 31%, engagement up to 71%, and the first conversion signals appearing on the Tableau comparison page. The blockers are not media, they're measurement and concentration: (1) close the tracking gap — reconcile the Bing pixel (1) against GA4 (4) and stand up a Bing LeadSource in Salesforce so we can see pipeline, not guess at it; (2) de-risk Compete-US, which is 70% of spend on unproven-in-pixel conversions — hold budget flat until the tracking is trustworthy rather than pouring in more; (3) add the four negatives to stop close-variant drift; (4) lift Brand IS from 79% toward 100% — cheap, defensive, and it's where the only confirmed conversion came from. Do not scale spend until we can answer "how many Bing leads reached Salesforce." Reallocate the ~$38 of weekly close-variant waste into brand defense.
