Ignite UI React — Weekly Paid Brief

Report date Fri 24 Jul 2026 WoW window Jul 16–22 vs Jul 9–15 Channel Google Ads only Owner Poojitha Battala

The account is spending well and reaching demand — but its measurement layer went dark in June, and that now sits above every other problem. Google Ads is delivering: React spend of $4,618 last week bought 1,741 clicks at a $2.65 CPC across seven cleanly-labeled React campaigns, and npm installs — our north star — rose 19% month over month (charts +51%) with no down month. Demand is real and adoption is climbing.

But GA4 paid-React sessions collapsed from ~5,834 in May to 491 in June to 165 so far in July. That is not a demand problem — clicks are up 46% WoW — it is a tracking break (GCLID auto-tagging / GA4 tag / conversion import), almost certainly tied to a late-May change. It hides CPA, and it means Salesforce shows no paid-search lead source at all. We are flying blind on everything downstream of the click.

Where we focus this week: (1) restore tracking — nothing else can be judged until it's back; (2) fix "react grid" ad rank — we lose 74% of the top slot to rank, not budget; (3) route paid traffic off the generic product page — 94% of it lands on /ignite-ui-react instead of the grid/charts/table pages it searched for. Fund Compete (losing 24% of its top slot to budget); hold and audit the new Demand Gen campaign.

Verdict
FIX — restore measurement before any scale or cut decision

Do not cut React paid. The demand-capture engine is healthy (clicks +46% WoW, npm +19% MoM, engagement 52–75% where visible), and the campaigns are cleanly structured. But the account has been economically un-measurable since June — no session attribution, no lead capture, no pipeline. Reallocating budget on today's numbers would be a decision made blind. Sequence: (1) repair GCLID→GA4→conversion import and CRM lead source; (2) re-route paid traffic to intent pages and fix "react grid" rank; (3) re-evaluate scale vs cut in 2–3 weeks on trustworthy data. Fund Compete now (its ROI signal — the account's only conversions — is the clearest we have).
