Executive read

Audience: Dean + leadership

Blazor paid is spending steadily but measuring nothing on the ad side. This week we put $1,994 to work across Google ($1,772) and the newly-live Bing account ($222) and generated 271 clicks of mostly high-intent, competitor-conquest demand. The engine is running more efficiently than last week — search CPCs down a fifth, CTR up — but Google Ads still records zero conversions and GA4 sees almost none of the paid clicks, so we cannot yet tie a dollar of spend to a trial or a sale.

Because the ad platforms can't see outcomes, we measure Blazor success by NuGet adoption instead. This is our first tracked week: the six public Infragistics Blazor packages sit at 107,206 cumulative downloads. From next Monday we report the weekly delta; today is the baseline. The honest framing for leadership: the media is getting cheaper and the demand is real, but until conversion tracking is wired we are optimizing to clicks and downloads, not revenue. Fixing tracking is the single highest-leverage move on this account.

The attribution gap, quantified

This week's 271 paid clicks should have produced a comparable count of GA4 cpc Blazor sessions. Instead GA4 attributes ~10 over the entire last 30 days. That is not a traffic problem — it's a measurement break: GCLID auto-tagging, landing-page redirects stripping parameters, or consent-mode loss. Every downstream number (CPA, ROAS, asset grade, conversion rate) inherits this hole.

Decisive verdict
  • FIX first (P1): repair click → session → conversion tracking and import a real key event (trial start / NuGet-feed page / SDK download). Nothing else can be optimized properly until this lands.
  • KEEP & fund: Compete and Components on Google — cheap efficient conquest, currently budget-throttled. Lift budgets to recover the ~22% lost impressions.
  • SCALE cautiously: Bing — $3 CPCs are too good to ignore; extend to Components/Charts themes once we confirm quality.
  • REALLOCATE: the ~$400/wk of tutorial-intent waste on Components → toward funded high-intent terms; hold Demand Gen flat until it shows up in GA4.

Honesty rule: NuGet downloads are cumulative-derived weekly deltas — they include CI/restore noise and inflate on release weeks as a new version ships (26.1.51 is the current build). They are a blended adoption signal, not 1:1 paid-attributed. Treat them as the north-star outcome, not a conversion count.

Shared paid-media glossary + Blazor-specific terms
Shared paid-media glossary

Impression share (IS)

The % of available impressions your ads actually won. 90% target = you show almost every time someone searches your term.

Budget-lost IS

Share of impressions missed because the budget ran out. High = raise budget to capture demand you're already qualified to win.

Rank-lost IS

Impressions missed because Ad Rank (bid × Quality Score) was too low. Fix with relevance/landing pages/bids, not budget.

CTR

Click-through rate = clicks ÷ impressions. Signals ad relevance to the query.

CPC

Cost per click. On premium dev terms $25–$35 is normal; conquest terms run cheaper.

Conquest / Compete

Bidding on a competitor's brand term to intercept their buyers. Labeled RESEARCH; our Compete campaign.

Demand Gen (DG)

Google's image/video audience product. Cheap clicks but placement-leaky; dilutes blended CTR/CPC — always read Search-only alongside.

Close-variant leakage

An Exact keyword matching a near-but-wrong query (or serving from the wrong campaign). Catch with search-term audits + cross-negatives.

Ours — Blazor-specific

NuGet downloads (north-star)

Weekly delta of summed cumulative totalDownloads across the public Infragistics Blazor packages, diffed Monday-to-Monday from snapshots. Replaces a lead goal — Blazor is a .NET/NuGet product.
