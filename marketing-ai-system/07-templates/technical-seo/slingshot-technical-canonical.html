SECTION 2

What changed since last crawl

Small cleanup, no new fires — and three named sources behind almost all the noise.

  • 44 fewer error pages and 287 fewer notices; health held at 46.
  • Broken internal pages (4xx) down 35; broken redirects down 4.
  • No new types of error appeared — the mix is the same debt as last week.

Where the error count actually comes from — three root causes, not thousands of unrelated problems:

1. Blocked language pages (the biggest driver)

The Japanese, Spanish, Korean and Portuguese sections (/ja/ /es/ /ko/ /pt-BR/) return "403 Forbidden" — a locked door — on hundreds of pages the site itself links to. This single fault also creates most of the multi-language-tag errors.

2. Old help-centre URLs

Knowledge-base pages in the old /en/…​.html shape bounce through a redirect to their clean address instead of pointing straight at it, and a handful throw a 500 server error.

3. A webinar link bug

A template is building webinar links with the query part mangled (%3Ftopic=…?fr=1), inventing URLs that don't exist and then dead-ending — this is most of the "broken redirects."

Is this our fault? Yes — all three are our own configuration and template issues, not a Google penalty or anything external. That's the good news: they're fully in our control, and fixing them can't hurt rankings — the pages that rank aren't the ones breaking.

SECTION 6

Cheat sheet — plain-English glossary

What the terms in this report actually mean.

Crawl
When a search engine's robot visits your pages to read them. Our audit is a crawl by Ahrefs' robot.
Health score
Ahrefs' 0–100 rating of how clean the site looks technically. Higher is better; 46 means lots of fixable debt.
404 (Not Found)
The page doesn't exist. A dead link.
403 (Forbidden)
The page exists but the server refuses to show it — a locked door. Worse than a 404 because we're blocking our own content.
500 (Server Error)
The page broke while loading — the server crashed on it.
Redirect (301 / 302)
Sends a visitor from an old URL to a new one. 301 = permanent (passes ranking value); 302 = temporary.
Broken redirect
A redirect that ends at a dead or forbidden page instead of a working one.
Redirect chain
Several redirects in a row before reaching the real page. Slows crawling and leaks value.
Canonical
A tag naming the "official" version of a page when duplicates exist, so Google credits the right one.
hreflang
A tag telling Google which language/country version of a page to show. Broken ones confuse it.
Indexing
A page being stored in Google's library so it can appear in results. No index = no traffic.
Crawl budget
The limited number of pages Google will crawl per visit. Broken URLs waste it.
Orphan page
A page with no internal links pointing to it, so it's hard to find and rarely crawled.
Trailing slash
The "/" at the end of a URL. With and without it count as two different pages unless you standardise.
